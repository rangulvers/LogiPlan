01 The problem

A layout looks fine on paper. Then the forklifts meet at a junction.

In-plant logistics is where good floor plans go wrong: a dock on the main aisle, one charger for six trucks, a door that is never free when the truck arrives. These are not drawing problems. They are problems of time and traffic, and a drawing cannot show time.

  • How many vehicles do I need?

    Run the plant with one, then two, then three. Compare how busy they are and how long loads wait. The answer is where adding another stops helping.

  • Where will it jam?

    Vehicles queue at junctions, wait for docks and block each other in dead ends. A traffic heatmap and per-junction waits show where, and why.

  • What really limits the plant?

    A machine, a dock door, a forklift, a charger? LogiPlan names the bottleneck in plain language and shows the evidence behind it.

The usual ways to answer these questions are a spreadsheet, a Lego mock-up, or the first week of production. LogiPlan stands in for the mock-up, and keeps the part of it that made it useful: you can see and touch the plant.

02 How it works

Four steps from an empty baseplate to an answer.

The pictures below are screenshots of the real planner, not mock-ups.

  1. 1

    Build the plant

    Lay road plates, one-way lanes and slow zones, then snap down bricks: Goods in, Workstation, Storage, Goods out, Parking and charging. Roads stay straight while you drag. Everything can be undone, and the baseplate grows as your plant does, up to 320 × 320 cells.

    A station docks on the road cells that touch it, so where you put it decides where vehicles stand and wait.

  2. 2

    Describe the work

    Draw arrows between stations to say where loads go next, in what share, how many a process consumes per cycle, in what batch size and priority. A Goods in can receive trucks on a rate or on a timetable you paste from Excel, through dock doors and a gate.

    Flows decide where loads go. Vehicles are not tied to stations.

  3. 3

    Add vehicles

    AGVs, forklifts, tugger trains or your own kind (4 presets to start from): speed, acceleration, capacity, load and unload time, batteries and chargers, breakdowns, and where they park. Any free vehicle serves any flow, nearest job first, oldest first, or balanced, as you choose.

  4. 4

    Press play

    The simulation runs from 1× to 1200× real time. Vehicles queue at junctions, reverse at dead ends, charge when their battery runs low and sometimes break down. Change demand, vehicle speed or process time while it runs, and watch what moves.

04 Evidence

From a moving picture to numbers you can defend.

Watching vehicles is persuasive. Counting is convincing. LogiPlan does both, and every number says how it is counted.

A dashboard that names the bottleneck

Throughput, lead time, work in progress, utilisation and time stuck in traffic, per station and per fleet, with findings in plain sentences that point at the limiting station and say what waits in front of it.

A heatmap of where traffic hurts

See which road cells carry the most vehicles and which ones make them wait. A Jobs overlay shows where every vehicle is heading right now.

Click anything for its statistics

Click a station, a flow, a road cell or a fleet and a panel opens with the numbers a planner asks first, each with an (i) that states exactly how it is counted. Click a vehicle and the plan draws where it usually drives: line width is trips, colour is time lost waiting, dashes are empty runs.

Trucks, gates and dock doors

Trucks arrive at a rate or on your timetable, check in at a gate, are unloaded by your forklifts and leave. Results shows the gate wait and the door time, and says whether the doors or the forklifts are the limit.

05 Decide

Keep several plans. Compare them fairly. Hand over the result.

A plan is rarely right on the first try, and rarely the only candidate. So LogiPlan is built around variants.

  • Variants A, B, C… of one plant, compared side by side with repeated runs, so one lucky seed does not decide.
  • Sweeps answer “how many AGVs do I need?” by running the plant again and again with a changing setting.
  • A coach tells you what a plant still needs, such as a Goods in that is not connected yet, and offers the fix in one click.
  • Reports and links: a self-contained HTML, print or PDF report, a PNG of the layout, the project as JSON, or a share link.

06 Value

What it is worth, as reasoning you can check.

There is no savings percentage on this page, because one would have to be invented. Here is the logic instead.

  1. Questions get answered before the money is spent.

    Moving a dock or adding a vehicle on the floor takes time and money, and often stops work. Moving a brick takes a second, and a simulated shift takes seconds of computing time (the demo above measures it on your machine). The cheapest moment to be wrong is the one before anything is built.

  2. The answer comes with its evidence.

    A number without a counting rule is an opinion. Every figure in the Statistics panel has an (i) that says what is counted and over what window, and calls a measurement indicative when it covers too little time. The same plant and seed always give the same results, so a colleague can reproduce yours.

  3. It teaches the physics, not just the tool.

    The examples ladder starts with a single forklift whose capacity you can work out on paper (“A vehicle is a machine with a cycle time, so its capacity can be calculated.”), then lets the simulation confirm it and show what the paper calculation missed: queues, charging, docks that cannot share the work.

  4. It is open, so you can look inside.

    The model, the traffic rules and the statistics are ordinary JavaScript under the MIT licence. If a number looks odd, you can read how it was made, and the tests that pin it.

A real example from the app

Dock lab: one street, three docks

Trucks bring pallets to a Goods in with three docks, each on its own short side road, and forklifts carry them to a Storage. Turn the three side roads into one row and the docks stop sharing the work: in the app’s own numbers the first dock takes almost all the visits and the others stand empty, because a forklift cannot drive past a parked one. Both layouts have three docks; only one of them shares the work. That is the kind of mistake that is cheap to find here and expensive to find on day one.

08 Under the hood

A simulation core you can run anywhere, and a UI that stays out of its way.

The engine has no idea there is a browser. It is plain data in, plain results out, which is why the same code runs in the planner, in the tests, in headless experiments run from Node and in the demo above.

  1. Model

    The plant as plain data, with mutators, validation, serialisation and the example plants. 15+ modules

  2. Simulation

    Road graph, collision-free traffic with junction blocking and deadlock detection, logistics, statistics, insights and the engine. No DOM. 25+ modules

  3. Store

    Undo and redo, variants and autosave: the application state around a plant.

  4. Interface

    A canvas renderer, the editor tools, panels, dashboard and reports. 70+ modules

Deterministic by construction

One seeded random generator, forked per subsystem. The same plant and seed give the same results, bit for bit. Golden tests pin the full results of the original example plants and of several test plants, so a change that was meant to leave those alone cannot alter them unnoticed.

No build step, no runtime dependencies

Vanilla ES modules served as they are. The site is a folder of static files, published by a workflow to GitHub Pages. An import checker fails the build if a name is imported that is not exported.

Tested in tiers

Unit and integration tests, seeded fuzz tests of the traffic rules and the logistics, and performance bounds. A fast tier runs on every change, the slow tests run in parallel shards in CI, and a test proves that every test file runs in exactly one job.

Versioned and honest about it

The planner shows the version and build it is running, tells you when a newer one is live without ever reloading by itself, and stamps its reports with the version that made them. The version at the time of writing is v0.6.0.

This page follows its own rules

It loads nothing from anywhere else: no web fonts, no scripts or images from third parties, no analytics, no cookies. The only outgoing links are to the repository and its licence. The numbers above are generated from the repository by a script and compared with this text by a test, so they cannot drift without the build failing. The demo imports the same engine modules as the planner.

09 Honest limits

What LogiPlan does not do, yet.

A planning aid with simplified models is only useful if you know where the simplifications are. Check important decisions against real data before you build.

Not in the product

  • Order picking. Trucks, docks, forklifts and storage are modelled; people picking items from shelves are not.
  • A multi-site model. “Two plants, one yard” is two plants on one baseplate with a shared warehouse and charging hall. It is a single plant model.
  • Racks, shift calendars and demand profiles. They are on the roadmap in docs/WAREHOUSE-DESIGN.md, not in the planner. A clock exists only for truck timetables.
  • Pedestrians and traffic lights.

Built, with a caveat

  • Trucks are events at a door, not vehicles on the road. A door is a count, not a place on the wall.
  • Defaults are indicative. A truck carries 24 pallets and check-in takes 5 minutes until you say otherwise.
  • Browsers. Verified in Chromium. Firefox and Safari should work but have not been tested, and touch screens have not been checked.
  • Very large plants run live at a fraction of the usual speed, and variant comparisons run on the main thread. README.md, “Known limits”, has the measurements.
